THE QUESTION

What does this specific decision-maker value, what are the real alternatives, and where do we create enough relative advantage to change the decision?

FLAGSHIP SKILL

Relative Value Strategy

A buyer-specific strategy engine for product, positioning, pricing, portfolio, market-entry, and executive decisions. It is designed to separate real relative premium from capability that is merely relevant.

01

Start with the decision

Define who is choosing, what they are trying to achieve, and which resource is actually being allocated.

02

Compare real alternatives

Include the incumbent, internal build, workarounds, decomposition into multiple vendors or hires, delay, and reallocation.

03

Find relative premium

Weight the value drivers that matter to this buyer and identify where your advantage is both important and hard to substitute.

04

Run the Elephant Check

Put human incentives back into the model. Saved money, time, authority, inventory, or budget does not disappear by default.

BUILT-IN STRESS TEST

The Elephant Anti-Pattern

A strategy assumes an actor will stop spending, surrender economics, abandon an incentive, cooperate, or behave “rationally” merely because the proposed system makes that behavior globally efficient.

What will the human or institution actually do with the freed resource?

THE STANDARD

A strategy should survive its strongest alternative and the people inside the system.

If the thesis only works because competitors are ignored, customers value what we happen to sell, or actors voluntarily surrender their incentives, it is not finished.
